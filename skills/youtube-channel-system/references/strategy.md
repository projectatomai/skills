# Channel strategy and niche selection

## Full channel plan

Produce an operating brief linking these decisions:

- **Viewer and promise:** who watches, the situation that triggers watching, what they gain, and why this creator can deliver it.
- **Positioning:** subject boundary, distinctive perspective, credible proof, voice, and subjects to avoid. Express the core promise in one sentence.
- **Content architecture:** a few distinct pillars, each with a repeatable series, viewer need, format, and example episode. Explain how episodes lead naturally to another video.
- **Discovery:** the intended role of search, browse, suggested, and Shorts for this audience; label these as strategy hypotheses rather than distribution guarantees.
- **Cadence and capacity:** hours available, time per episode, constraints, batching opportunities, and a sustainable publishing schedule. Include slack for revisions. Keep Shorts and long-form goals distinct.
- **First month:** a weekly calendar connecting topics, packaging, research, scripting, production, release, and review. Name a fallback if capacity proves lower than expected.
- **Growth and revenue:** select initial experiments and appropriate monetization stages using the analytics/monetization reference if needed.

Use user-provided channel facts. For an established channel, preserve successful identity and formats unless evidence supports changing them. Prefer a small pilot over an unsupported total rebrand.

## Niche research

Compare plausible options against creator interest/expertise, production feasibility, audience needs, observable demand, competition, differentiation, topic depth, longevity, and revenue fit. Keep evidence and judgment separate. Research relevant channels and recent uploads; record URLs, observation dates, formats, recurring topics, and visible performance. Compare video age and channel scale before calling an upload an outlier. Avoid selecting only exceptional winners.

A useful matrix rates factors from 1–5 with explicit anchors and brief evidence. Default to equal weighting unless the user's goals justify another choice; explain any chosen weights. Treat totals as decision aids, not probabilities of success. Missing evidence should be marked unknown, not assigned a confident score.

Recommend one primary niche and a viable alternative, explaining tradeoffs and a low-cost validation pilot. Define a target viewer and defensible content gap. Do not imply that high visible views prove an underserved or profitable niche.

## Ranked idea backlog

For each idea include working title, target viewer need, pillar, distinct angle, format, intended discovery surface, effort, evidence or hypothesis, and priority. Rank using a transparent mix of audience fit, demand evidence, differentiation, series potential, and feasibility. Label scores as editorial judgments.

If the user requests the first 100 ideas, deliver 100 genuinely distinct ideas grouped by pillar and ranked consistently; flag the first production candidates. Otherwise favor a smaller usable backlog. Avoid padding with near-identical titles or presenting idea counts as strategy.
