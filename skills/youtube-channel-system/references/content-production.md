# Content packaging and production

## Episode package

Start from a one-sentence promise and a concrete viewer need. For a complete episode package, deliver:

1. A concept brief: audience, angle, viewer payoff, intended format/runtime, evidence required, and why the idea may work.
2. Several distinct title directions with complementary thumbnail concepts. Specify focal subject, composition, emotion or contrast, and minimal optional text. Titles and visuals must truthfully represent the video.
3. Alternative opening hooks, then a selected opening that quickly demonstrates relevance, stakes, and credible payoff. Avoid lengthy greetings or empty hype.
4. A narrative outline with approximate timing, demonstration or evidence beats, transitions, and payoff. Open questions should be resolved rather than used to delay value.
5. The full spoken script when requested, with visual/editing notes separated clearly. Mark unavailable facts or quotes for research instead of inventing them. Check approximate runtime using a stated speaking-rate assumption.
6. A context-appropriate CTA and a natural next-video path.

Explain packaging and retention choices briefly. A single-video request should receive a finished usable package, not a generic lecture. Preserve the creator's voice. Avoid treating frequent cuts, sensationalism, or arbitrary pattern interrupts as universally effective.

## Production workflow

Map research → brief → packaging → script → record/voice → edit → thumbnail → quality review → upload preparation → publication → review. For each relevant stage specify input, output, owner, approximate effort, and completion criteria. Choose tools around existing access and budget; verify current prices/capabilities before recommending purchases. Tool access must not be assumed.

Use a reusable episode brief containing: viewer, promise, title/thumbnail direction, source list, outline, runtime, required shots/assets, CTA, due date, and experiment. Build a simple production board with statuses, next action, owner, and blocker when managing multiple episodes.

Batch compatible work without committing the entire backlog before testing the initial videos. Estimate total workload against actual weekly capacity. Measure time saved against a baseline before claiming a speed improvement.

Quality checks should address the real format: accurate claims, original contribution, permissions for source material/music, clear voice and audio, readable graphics/captions, promise/payoff alignment, pacing, export playback, and accurate upload metadata. Check current platform disclosure rules when synthetic content or sponsorships require them.

## Repurposing

Identify self-contained moments with their own hook, context, and payoff. Supply cut timestamps when footage/transcripts support them; otherwise provide proposed excerpt boundaries. Adapt framing, captions, opening, and CTA to the destination instead of assuming a cropped long-form video works everywhere. Do not promise Shorts viewers will convert to long-form viewers; treat that as a measurable hypothesis.

If publication is requested and authorized, use available tools, verify the actual resulting status/URL, and stop on ambiguous submission state rather than repeating a potentially successful upload.
