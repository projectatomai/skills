# Music-led film

Use the track the person chose. Do not swap in a generated alternative. Get their image choices, or their permission to curate. Favorites can feed a shortlist for them to confirm. Do not force a story or recurring characters from earlier, replaced work.

## Analyze and pace

Keep the original music file untouched and work on a copy. Probe its duration, sample rate and channels. Listen, and read the waveform, alongside beat and onset detection. Map phrases, accents, builds, quiet passages, transitions and the ending. One tempo number is not enough for ambient or changing music. Save a timecoded map and mark the detections you are unsure of.

Plan independent shots around that map. Alternate scale, material, light and movement on purpose: a vast space, then a close texture. Stillness, then a creature moving. Warm metal, then cool stone. Let shots breathe across beats, and cut or reveal on musical events that matter, not on every beat.

## Animate the selected sources

For each shot, record: the source image and its id, music in and out, duration, the subject's action, motion in the setting, camera motion, the transition, and what must stay unchanged.

- Keep figures readable and their scale stable.
- A still of a walking pose is not a walking shot.
- Do not quietly replace requested subject animation with a slow zoom.
- Use the video tool and model the person chose. A request for stills does not authorize video or paid compute.
- Check the registry before animating any image, as the main skill describes.

Apply `video-motion-checks.md` to every clip. Also look at anatomy, footfalls and contact, texture stability, flicker, faithfulness to the source and whether the ending is usable. Record failures and approved retries. Do not multiply generations silently.

## A playable film and an editable timeline

Build a master timeline with the correct frame rate and source durations. Keep the music continuous. Do not cut or loop it without a reason the person agreed to. Use the final aspect ratio, resolution and length they chose, which may differ from the stills.

Deliver a playable render, plus an editable interchange file such as FCPXML with the media organized beside it. Use valid encoded paths, exact rational time values and readable clip names. Check the file's structure and that every referenced file exists, and import it into the editor when one is available. A file that is valid XML is not the same as a successful import, so say which you checked.

Check playback, duration, that audio is present, several musical accents, each transition, black gaps, missing media, and the first and last seconds. Look at frames and clips, not only at exit codes. Give full paths to the movie, the editable project and the media, and say plainly what you could not verify.

Keep notes for next time: the chosen music, the shots, the clips, timeline and export paths, and what remains.
