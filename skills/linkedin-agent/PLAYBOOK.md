# PLAYBOOK.md: formats, opening lines, structure and review

The examples are invented. Use the shape, not the words.

## Post formats

Pick a format on purpose. Never present three drafts in one format with different wording.

- **Story.** A small real moment with a setup, a turn and a landing. "I nearly sent a quote that would have lost us money. One line in the client's email stopped me."
- **Contrarian take.** Argue against a common belief and give one specific reason. "Everyone says to answer leads within five minutes. Our best clients this year waited a day for a proper answer."
- **One lesson.** One thing learned, tied to one situation. "After Thursday I have a new rule: never demo from the machine that runs the build."
- **Practical tip.** Something a reader can use today. "If your sign-up form has more than three required fields, remove one before tomorrow."
- **A standard.** A public line about quality or behavior. "If your home page cannot say what the product is in two seconds, it has not launched."
- **A day as it was.** What the work really looked like, with specifics. "Shipped nothing today. Six hours tracing a sync bug to one timestamp. That is the job."
- **Small observation.** A lived detail that shows a real person. "The office plant has outlasted three roadmaps."

## Opening lines

The first line has to earn the second. Types that work:

- **A specific number.** "We ran four experiments last month. Three were a waste."
- **A contrary claim.** "Most onboarding advice is wrong for small teams."
- **A confession.** "I have not looked at our churn number in two weeks."
- **A scene.** "It is 11:40 at night and the build has failed for the third time."
- **Direct address.** "If you run a team of five, this one is for you."
- **The cost of a mistake.** "A missing index cost us forty minutes of downtime."
- **An unflattering truth.** "I have called it a beta for seven months. It is not a beta."
- **A quiet observation.** "The best demo I saw this month was one screen and no slides."

## Length

- **Short, 50 to 150 words.** The default. One idea, no subheadings.
- **Medium, 150 to 300 words.** Lessons and stories that need a setup, a turn and a landing.
- **Long, up to 500 words.** Only when every line earns its place. If a fifth can be cut without losing the point, it was too long.

Never stretch a short idea into a long post.

## Worth posting

- Practical value from work the person really did.
- What building something is really like, with numbers, timing and names they are free to share.
- Honest progress and honest reversals.
- Clear standards about products, demos and behavior.
- Distinctions and trade-offs a reader can apply quickly.

## Not worth posting

- A summary of a news story with nothing added.
- Career advice anyone could have written.
- Commentary with no sharp reason to exist.
- A polished mini-essay with no tension and no claim.
- Anything written for engagement first and truth second.

## Endings

In order of preference:

1. **A specific question** tied to a detail in the post.
2. **A general question**, only when the post is broad enough for it to mean something.
3. **No call to action.** Often best for short posts.

Never end on "What do you think?".

## Review checklist

Walk every draft through these in order. Mark each pass or fail and quote the failing text.

1. Does the first line pull the reader in, or is it a warm-up?
2. Is there one specific detail (a number, a name, a moment) a reader can picture?
3. Is there a real opinion, or only neutral reporting?
4. Does the length match how much there is to say?
5. Does it end on something specific, or fade out?
6. Does it break any hard rule in `VOICE.md`?
7. Are there at most three hashtags, all at the end?
8. Would the person say this out loud?
9. Can any sentence be cut without losing the point?
10. If there is a link, is it in the post's link field and not in the body text?
