# YouTube packaging and upload

Read this before writing release text or uploading. "Viral" is a hope, never a promise.

## Research for each release

Read YouTube's current help pages on titles, thumbnails, descriptions and discovery, and save the links and the date you read them in the film's `publishing/youtube/` folder. Prefer YouTube's own guidance over folklore. If the person's channel analytics are available, look at comparable uploads. Keep platform facts, your observations and your guesses apart. Do not invent search volumes.

## Title, description, thumbnail

- **Title.** Draft a few candidates that really differ (the conflict, the emotional payoff, the searchable subject). Pick one and say why. Lead with the premise, keep it short and accurate, and put series branding later. No invented danger, awards or famous names.
- **Description.** The first lines state the real premise and why someone would watch. Add accurate credits and real links. Keep hashtags few and relevant.
- **Thumbnail.** It takes the film's orientation and aspect ratio. Design it with the title, and check both together at phone size. If it carries text, check the spelling letter by letter and that it reads at about 320 and 120 pixels wide. Keep text off faces and the corner where the duration sits. Confirm the saved file's dimensions.

A clear frame from the film can serve as the thumbnail. Generate new artwork only when asked.

## Settings that need care

- **Audience.** Set "made for kids" from the film's intended viewers and real content. Being animated does not settle it. Ask if the intent is unclear, and never choose a setting to unlock a feature.
- **AI disclosure.** Consider the whole production, including generated voices and music, against YouTube's current altered-content guidance. A credit in the description does not replace a required disclosure.
- **Visibility.** "Upload" means upload. When visibility was not stated, upload as private and say so. Public release, scheduling and notifications need the person's word.

## Upload and verify

1. Confirm the final export and its hash. Never upload a storyboard video or a replaced render.
2. Use a connected YouTube tool if the agent has one. Otherwise use the person's signed-in browser, or give them the package to upload. If the site is signed out, ask them to sign in. Never type their password.
3. Record the video id and link as soon as they exist. If interrupted, resume that upload. Check the channel's content list before uploading again.
4. Apply the title, description, thumbnail, audience, language and category. Leave unrelated channel settings alone. Do not dispute a rights claim automatically.
5. Wait for processing. Confirm duration, picture, sound and the highest processed resolution.
6. Save the agreed visibility, read the settings back from the page, and give the person the link and its visibility.

## Afterwards

Judge the packaging from impressions, traffic sources, click-through rate, early retention and watch time together, and not from the first few views. Keep the unused title candidates for a later test. Do not change a live title or set up monitoring unless asked.
